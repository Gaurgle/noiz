## 1. Seamless sample looper (highest priority)

### 1.1 What the samples actually contain

All three files: stereo, 44.1 kHz, PCM16, 970 200 frames = 22.00 s, peak 0.89 to 1.0, body RMS about 0.09.

| File | Last four 0.5 s RMS windows (20.0 to 22.0 s) | Digital silence at end | First frame |
|------|-----------------------------------------------|------------------------|-------------|
| light-rain | 0.059, 0.021, 0.003, 0.000 | 21.59 s to end (0.41 s) | 0.042, abrupt |
| calm-rain  | 0.083, 0.072, 0.032, 0.003 | none, but ~1 s fade | 0.214, abrupt |
| heavy-rain | 0.094, 0.060, 0.010, 0.002 | 21.54 s to end (0.46 s) | 0.088, abrupt |

Two facts follow:

1. **Every file has a baked-in fade-out of roughly 1.5 to 2 s and a hard, non-zero first sample.** They were exported from a longer recording with a fade applied at the tail but not the head.
2. light-rain is not stationary: it has gust bursts (0.289 RMS at 5.5 s, 0.260 at 12 s, 0.276 at 19 s) against a 0.05 to 0.06 floor. Its 19.0 to 19.5 s window is at 0.276, immediately before the loop region, while the head sits at 0.067. That is a 12 dB level step whatever crossfade you use, unless the crossfade is long enough to smear it or the loop point is moved.

### 1.2 Why the current `SamplePlayer` audibly fails

`src/samples.rs:75-117`, created with `crossfade_ms = 2000.0` at `src/audio.rs:231`.

**(a) The crossfade window sits exactly on the baked fade-out.** `xfade_len = 2000 ms = 88 200 frames`, so `loop_end = 970 200 - 88 200 = 882 000 = 20.0 s`. The blend is `tail * (1 - t) + head * t` for 20.0 to 22.0 s. But the tail is already decaying to zero in that same window, so the "tail" term contributes almost nothing after the first half second. The output is effectively `head * t`: a 2 s linear fade-in from near silence, once every 20 s. That is the pump the README calls "experimental". No crossfade math fixes this while the loop region overlaps the baked fade; the files must be trimmed (or re-exported without the fade) first.

**(b) Linear crossfade is the wrong curve for uncorrelated material.** Rain is noise-like: the tail and head are uncorrelated, so their powers add, not their amplitudes. With linear gains `(1 - t)` and `t`, summed power is `(1 - t)^2 + t^2`, which is 0.5 at the midpoint: a 3 dB dip in the middle of every crossfade even with perfect source material. Equal-power gains (`cos(t * pi/2)`, `sin(t * pi/2)`, or the cheaper `sqrt(1 - t)`, `sqrt(t)`) keep summed power at 1.0 throughout. The same error exists in the rain type switch: outgoing fades out linearly over 500 ms while incoming fades in linearly, another 3 dB dip on every `r` press.

**(c) The loop jump itself is continuous, so it is not the click source.** At the last blended frame `t = (xfade_len - 1) / xfade_len`, output is ~`head[xfade_len - 1]`; the next frame after `pos = xfade_len` is `head[xfade_len]`. No data is re-read and no sample is skipped. The effective loop length is `total - xfade_len = 20 s`. This part of the design is sound; keep the idea, just move it offline (see 1.3).

**(d) No resampling.** `next_stereo(target_rate)` uses `target_rate` only for the fade speed and consumes exactly one source frame per device frame. On a 48 kHz device (most USB DACs, many Bluetooth and HDMI paths, PipeWire default on Linux) the rain plays 8.8 % fast and 1.5 semitones high, and the 2 s crossfade becomes 1.84 s. On a 96 kHz device it plays an octave up. `audio.rs` takes `device.default_output_config()` and never looks at `Sample::sample_rate`. The `sample_rate` field on `SamplePlayer` is never read (clippy confirms).

**(e) The 500 ms fade-in starts at a hard first sample.** Masked at start by the envelope beginning at 0.0, but calm-rain's first sample is -0.21 and the whole blend region reads `data[0..]` at `t -> 0` weight, so it is harmless there. It becomes relevant only if you ever start playback at pos 0 without the envelope.

**(f) Rapid rain switching drops a voice mid-fade.** `src/audio.rs:223-227`: when `rain_pending` fires, the current player becomes `rain_outgoing`, overwriting any outgoing voice that is still fading. Pressing `r` twice within 500 ms hard-cuts the first voice at whatever amplitude it had: a real click, unrelated to looping.

**(g) Allocation and copy inside the realtime callback.** `SamplePlayer::from_preloaded` does `sample.data.clone()`: a 7.7 MB heap allocation plus memcpy (970 200 frames x 2 channels x 4 bytes) on the audio thread every time rain is switched on or changed. On a 512 frame buffer (11.6 ms at 44.1 kHz) this is very likely to cause an underrun, heard as a dropout or click right as the new rain starts. The old player's `Vec` is also freed on the audio thread when `rain_outgoing` is set to `None`.

**(h) Minor:** `let max = (1 << (bits - 1)) as f32` for PCM16 divides by 32768, fine. `xfade.min(total / 3)` caps the crossfade; fine. The per-frame `fade_speed` computation is cheap.

### 1.3 Recommended design: pre-bake a seamless loop at load time

Rationale: the loop content never changes at runtime, the files are preloaded anyway, and all the delicate math (trim, level match, equal-power blend, resample) then runs once on the main thread where allocation and `f64` are free. The audio callback becomes "read frame, increment, wrap", which is trivially click-free and trivially testable.

Pipeline per file, executed in `start_audio` before `build_output_stream` (the device rate is known at that point):

1. **Decode** to interleaved `f32` (existing `Sample::from_wav`).
2. **Trim the baked fade-out.** Compute a 50 ms RMS envelope, take the median over the file as the reference level, and cut the file at the last window whose RMS is at least 0.7 x median, minus one window of safety. For these files that lands around 20.0 to 20.5 s. Also drop the first 20 ms to get off the abrupt head (optional once a crossfade is in place, but cheap). Prefer re-exporting the source files without the fade; the trim step then becomes a no-op guard.
3. **Resample to the device rate** if `sample_rate != device_rate`. Linear interpolation is adequate for rain (no tonal content to alias audibly); `rubato` (`SincFixedIn`) if you want it right for any future tonal sample such as fire crackle or bells. Do this before baking so the crossfade length is in device frames.
4. **Bake the loop.** Choose `xfade` frames (see guidance). `loop_len = total - xfade`. Output buffer has `loop_len` frames:
   - frames `0..xfade`: `tail[i] * cos(theta) + head[i] * sin(theta)`, `theta = (i / xfade) * pi/2`, where `tail[i] = data[loop_len + i]` and `head[i] = data[i]`.
   - frames `xfade..loop_len`: `data[i]` unchanged.
   Continuity check: `out[loop_len - 1] = data[loop_len - 1]`, `out[0] = data[loop_len]` (theta = 0), consecutive source frames. `out[xfade - 1] ~ data[xfade - 1]` (theta ~ pi/2), `out[xfade] = data[xfade]`, consecutive. Both seams are sample-continuous by construction.
5. **Optionally normalise** each loop to a common RMS (e.g. -20 dBFS) so light/calm/heavy sit at comparable levels and the `rain_vol` slider means the same thing for each.
6. Store as `Arc<LoopSample>` and hand the array of `Arc`s to the callback. Starting a voice is then an `Arc::clone` (one atomic increment), not a 7.7 MB copy.

**Crossfade length guidance for noise-like material.** For stationary broadband noise 1 to 2 s is already inaudible with equal-power gains. For material with slow level variation (light-rain's gusts) the crossfade should be long enough that the level ramp between tail and head is slower than the natural gust dynamics: 4 to 6 s is a good default for a 20 s file (keep `xfade <= loop_len / 3` so most of the loop is untouched). Longer crossfades cost nothing at runtime because they are baked. If a file has a strong event near its end (light-rain at 19 s), either pick the loop point before it or accept that the event is heard once per loop; a crossfade cannot hide a 12 dB burst.

**Rain type switch.** Keep two voices, but give each voice an equal-power envelope (`sin`/`cos` of a 0..1 phase) and allow more than one outgoing voice (a small fixed array of, say, 3 voices; the oldest is stolen if all are busy). 500 ms is fine for the switch; 1 s sounds more natural for rain.

### 1.4 Code sketches

Load-time baking (main thread, allocation allowed):

```rust
pub struct LoopSample {
    pub frames: Box<[f32]>, // interleaved stereo, device rate, loop_len frames
    pub loop_len: usize,
}

fn rms_envelope(data: &[f32], ch: usize, win: usize) -> Vec<f32> {
    data.chunks(win * ch)
        .map(|c| (c.iter().map(|s| s * s).sum::<f32>() / c.len() as f32).sqrt())
        .collect()
}

/// Cut a baked fade-out: last window with RMS >= ratio * median, minus one window.
fn trim_tail(data: &mut Vec<f32>, ch: usize, rate: u32, ratio: f32) {
    let win = (rate as usize) / 20; // 50 ms
    let env = rms_envelope(data, ch, win);
    let mut sorted = env.clone();
    sorted.sort_by(|a, b| a.total_cmp(b));
    let median = sorted[sorted.len() / 2];
    let last_good = env.iter().rposition(|&e| e >= median * ratio).unwrap_or(env.len() - 1);
    let keep_frames = last_good.saturating_sub(1).max(1) * win;
    data.truncate(keep_frames * ch);
}

/// Linear-interpolating resampler. Good enough for rain; swap for rubato if needed.
fn resample_linear(data: &[f32], ch: usize, from: u32, to: u32) -> Vec<f32> {
    if from == to { return data.to_vec(); }
    let in_frames = data.len() / ch;
    let out_frames = (in_frames as f64 * to as f64 / from as f64) as usize;
    let step = from as f64 / to as f64;
    let mut out = Vec::with_capacity(out_frames * ch);
    for i in 0..out_frames {
        let pos = i as f64 * step;
        let i0 = pos as usize;
        let i1 = (i0 + 1).min(in_frames - 1);
        let frac = (pos - i0 as f64) as f32;
        for c in 0..ch {
            let a = data[i0 * ch + c];
            let b = data[i1 * ch + c];
            out.push(a + (b - a) * frac);
        }
    }
    out
}

pub fn bake_loop(sample: &Sample, device_rate: u32, xfade_secs: f32) -> LoopSample {
    let ch = sample.channels as usize;
    let mut data = sample.data.clone();
    trim_tail(&mut data, ch, sample.sample_rate, 0.7);
    let data = resample_linear(&data, ch, sample.sample_rate, device_rate);
    // Upmix mono to stereo once, so the callback never branches on channel count.
    let data: Vec<f32> = if ch == 1 { data.iter().flat_map(|&s| [s, s]).collect() } else { data };
    let total = data.len() / 2;
    let xfade = ((device_rate as f32 * xfade_secs) as usize).min(total / 3).max(1);
    let loop_len = total - xfade;
    let mut frames = vec![0.0f32; loop_len * 2];
    for i in 0..loop_len {
        for c in 0..2 {
            frames[i * 2 + c] = if i < xfade {
                let theta = (i as f32 / xfade as f32) * std::f32::consts::FRAC_PI_2;
                let (s, co) = theta.sin_cos();
                data[(loop_len + i) * 2 + c] * co + data[i * 2 + c] * s
            } else {
                data[i * 2 + c]
            };
        }
    }
    LoopSample { frames: frames.into_boxed_slice(), loop_len }
}
```

Runtime voice (audio thread, no allocation, no branches on channel count):

```rust
pub struct LoopVoice {
    sample: Arc<LoopSample>,
    pos: usize,
    env_phase: f32,       // 0..1, drives equal-power envelope
    env_step: f32,        // +1/(rate*secs) in, -1/(rate*secs) out
}

impl LoopVoice {
    pub fn start(sample: Arc<LoopSample>, rate: f32, fade_secs: f32) -> Self {
        Self { sample, pos: 0, env_phase: 0.0, env_step: 1.0 / (rate * fade_secs) }
    }
    pub fn release(&mut self, rate: f32, fade_secs: f32) {
        self.env_step = -1.0 / (rate * fade_secs);
    }
    pub fn finished(&self) -> bool { self.env_step < 0.0 && self.env_phase <= 0.0 }

    #[inline]
    pub fn next(&mut self) -> (f32, f32) {
        self.env_phase = (self.env_phase + self.env_step).clamp(0.0, 1.0);
        let g = (self.env_phase * std::f32::consts::FRAC_PI_2).sin(); // equal-power
        let i = self.pos * 2;
        let f = &self.sample.frames;
        let out = (f[i] * g, f[i + 1] * g);
        self.pos += 1;
        if self.pos == self.sample.loop_len { self.pos = 0; }
        out
    }
}
```

In `start_audio`: build `let rain_loops: [Option<Arc<LoopSample>>; 3]` before the stream using `config.sample_rate().0`; in the callback keep `let mut rain_voices: [Option<LoopVoice>; 3]`. On `rain_pending`: `release` every active voice, then start a new one in the first free slot (steal the oldest if none). Mixing: sum `voice.next()` over active voices. Drop finished voices with `= None`; dropping a `LoopVoice` only decrements the `Arc`, no deallocation while another reference exists in `rain_loops`.

If you would rather keep per-sample resampling at runtime (e.g. to support changing devices without rebake), replace `pos: usize` with `phase: f64`, `step = file_rate / device_rate`, and linearly interpolate between `floor(phase)` and `floor(phase) + 1` with wrap at `loop_len`. Pre-baking is simpler and I would start there.

### 1.5 How to verify

1. **Unit test on the seam (no audio device needed).** Build a `Sample` from a deterministic `SmallRng` white noise buffer of e.g. 3 s, call `bake_loop`, render 3 full loops into a `Vec<f32>` via `LoopVoice`, and assert:
   - `max |x[n] - x[n-1]|` in a 64 frame window around each loop boundary is <= `max |x[n] - x[n-1]|` over the whole body times 1.05 (a click shows up as an outlier delta).
   - 50 ms RMS over the rendered output has `min / median >= 0.8` (no pump).
   - `frames.len() == loop_len * 2` and `loop_len == total - xfade`.
   A second test with a synthetic baked fade (multiply the last 1 s by a linear ramp to 0) checks that `trim_tail` removes it and the RMS criterion still passes. These cost milliseconds and run in CI.
2. **Offline render for ears.** Add a hidden `noiz --render-rain calm --loops 4 out.wav` (or a `#[test] #[ignore]` writing into `target/`) using `hound::WavWriter`. Open in any editor (Audacity, `sox out.wav -n stat`, `ffmpeg -af astats`): look at the waveform at 20 s multiples; it should be impossible to find the seam visually.
3. **Listen test protocol.** Headphones, rain only, noise off, volume 50 %. Note the clock at start; listen through at least three loop points (about 60 s). Repeat on a 48 kHz device (set in Audio MIDI Setup on macOS) to confirm pitch and speed are unchanged from 44.1 kHz.
4. **Realtime safety check.** Run with `RUST_LOG`-free `cargo run --release` while hammering `r` ten times per second; no dropouts means the voice array and `Arc` approach holds.

## 2. Audio engine review (`src/audio.rs`)

**Realtime safety.** The callback is close to allocation-free for the noise and binaural paths, which is good. Violations:

- `samples::SamplePlayer::from_preloaded` clones 7.7 MB on the audio thread (line 231) and frees it (line 238). See 1.2 (g). This is the single most likely cause of audible glitches today.
- Six `Mutex<f32>` locks per callback (lines 187-192), one more per frame when fading (line 268), and one `lock()` **per frame** to publish `noise_vol` back to the TUI (line 251): roughly 44 100 lock/unlock pairs per second for a value the TUI reads at 10 Hz. `std::sync::Mutex` is uncontended-fast but not priority-inversion-safe; if the TUI thread is preempted while holding `target_noise_vol` the audio thread blocks. Replace every `Mutex<f32>` with `AtomicU32` storing `f32::to_bits`, read once per callback. A 20 line `AtomicF32` newtype covers all of them. `binaural_freq` is also written from the callback under lock (lines 211, 215) only so the TUI can display it; make it atomic as well.
- `eprintln!` in the error callback is fine (not the data callback).

**State sharing with the TUI.** The pattern "store value + set pending flag" (`noise_type` + `pending_switch`, etc.) is sound but uses `Relaxed` ordering for both, so the callback can theoretically see `pending_switch == true` before the new `noise_type`. In practice x86 and Apple Silicon make this a non-issue for a u8 written before a bool, but `Release` on the store and `Acquire` on the load costs nothing and makes it correct. Alternatively drop the pending flags entirely and have the callback compare the atomic against a locally cached last value.

**Sample format.** `build_output_stream` is built only for `f32`. `default_output_config()` can report `I16` or `U16` on Linux ALSA hardware; then `build_output_stream` fails with "failed to build audio stream" and noiz exits. Match on `config.sample_format()` and build the stream generically (`fn run<T: cpal::SizedSample + cpal::FromSample<f32>>`), writing via `T::from_sample(x)`.

**Resampling.** None; see 1.2 (d). Noise and binaural are rate-correct because they use `sample_rate` directly.

**Click-free parameter changes.**
- `target_noise_vol` is slewed over 100 ms: good.
- `bin_vol`, `rain_vol`, `lfo_depth`, `binaural_base` are applied per callback with no slew. Steps are 1 % / 2 Hz, so zipper noise is at the edge of audibility; a one-pole smoother (`v += (target - v) * 0.001`) per frame fixes it for free.
- `binaural_base` changes are phase-continuous (phase accumulators), so no click there: good.
- The 1.5 s per-generator ramps and the `NoiseGen` "always warm" approach are good; the noise type switch is genuinely seamless.
- The pause gate at line 373 zeroes output and `continue`s, skipping generator and LFO updates. When the generators resume they continue from the old state so no click, but the LFO phase stops advancing while paused; cosmetic.
- Envelopes are all linear. For the 5 s timer fade-out a perceptually linear (dB) curve would sound more even; optional.

**Volume smoothing / loudness.** Pink is `(sum of 17 uniforms) / 17` with std about 0.14, white is `0.35 * uniform` (std 0.20), brown is clamped to 0.8 then lowpassed. Loudness is not matched across types; switching white to pink is a noticeable level drop. Worth a one-time calibration constant per type.

**Denormals.** `BrownNoise.lp`, `PinkNoise.running_sum` and the envelope variables cannot hover near denormal because they are driven by uniform random input or clamp at 0.0/1.0. Not a practical concern here; if you later add IIR EQ, add a tiny DC offset or use flush-to-zero on the audio thread.

**Buffer size.** Default (`BufferSize::Default`), typically 512 frames on CoreAudio. Fine for this app; there is no low-latency requirement. If Linux users report crackle, exposing `--buffer-size` is a cheap escape hatch.

**Channel handling.** The `match channels { 1 => ..., _ => { ... if channels > 1 ... } }` at line 426 has a redundant inner check. Devices with more than 2 channels get silence on channels 3+, which is right.

**Smaller observations.**
- Line 14 comment says `0=white, 1=pink, 2=brown`; the actual mapping is `0=off, 1=white, 2=pink, 3=brown` (line 46).
- Line 53 comment says 300 Hz default carrier but the value is 140.0; README says per-preset carriers of 200 to 400 Hz, which the code does not implement (single carrier for all presets).
- `binaural_split` (line 188) is read and never used; `bin_active_split` is the real source.
- `fade_out` completing sets `paused = true` from the audio thread; the TUI then reads `paused` to display state. Works, but the "who owns `paused`" story is split across threads.
- `rain_outgoing.as_ref().map_or(false, ...)` is clippy's `is_some_and`.

## 3. Feature upgrade candidates

Effort: S = under a day, M = a few days, L = a week or more.

**Generic sample-slot system (fire, wind, cafe, ocean).** (M) The rain code is hard-wired to three indices and one row. Replace `rain_type: AtomicU8` with a small table of `Slot { name, files: Vec<PathBuf>, loop: Option<Arc<LoopSample>> }` discovered at startup, and generalise the TUI row to iterate over slots. The looper from section 1 is category-agnostic. Do this after the looper is solid, otherwise every new category inherits the pump.

**User sample directories (XDG).** (S) `samples_dir()` looks next to the executable and in cwd, which is why rain only works from the repo checkout and silently does nothing after `cargo install`. Search `$XDG_DATA_HOME/noiz/samples` (`~/Library/Application Support/noiz/samples` on macOS via the `directories` crate), then `$NOIZ_SAMPLES`, then the current fallbacks. Show "no samples found: put wavs in ..." in the rain row instead of a dead toggle.

**Embedding samples or a download step.** (S to M) `include_bytes!` of three 3.7 MB files gives a 12 MB binary and zero setup; convert to mono or 16 kHz if size matters, or ship as OGG/FLAC and add a decoder (`symphonia`, M). Alternative: `noiz samples fetch` pulling a tagged release asset into the XDG dir (M, needs hosting and a hash check). Embedding is the lowest-friction fix for the "only works from the repo" problem.

**Config file and presets.** (M) A `config.toml` in the XDG config dir with default volumes, default noise, carrier, LFO depth; named presets as `[preset.focus]` tables; `noiz focus` loads one; `s` in the TUI saves current state. `serde` + `toml` dependencies. Pairs with CLI flags below.

**CLI flags for every TUI control.** (S) `--bin alpha --rain calm --rain-vol 0.4 --carrier 200 --mod 0.1 --compact`. `clap` derive already in place; mostly wiring in `main.rs` plus making initial state set the pending flags.

**Mixing multiple samples.** (S once slots exist) Allow more than one slot active with its own volume. The voice array from section 1 already supports N voices; the cost is TUI rows and a per-slot volume atomic.

**EQ / lowpass on noise.** (S) A single biquad lowpass (or tilt) on the noise bus with cutoff on a TUI row. 30 lines of DSP; add denormal guard. Brown already has a one-pole, so the natural shape is "move that to a shared, adjustable filter".

**Pomodoro cycles.** (M) Timer today is a single countdown. A work/break schedule needs a small state machine in `tui.rs` (which is one more reason to split that file first), different end signals for work vs break, and a count display.

**System notifications on timer end.** (S) `notify-rust` works on macOS and Linux (`osascript` fallback on macOS if you want zero deps). Fire alongside the morse signal.

**Distribution: Homebrew tap, cargo-binstall, release CI.** (M) GitHub Actions matrix (macos-latest, ubuntu-latest with `libasound2-dev`), `cargo build --release`, upload tarballs on tag, `cargo-dist` automates most of it including a Homebrew formula and binstall metadata. Embedding samples first makes the binary self-contained, which distribution needs.

**Tests.** (S to start) There are zero tests. Cheapest high-value set: `parse_duration`, `adjust_timer_by`, `next_row`, `bake_loop` seam and RMS tests from 1.5, and a `NoiseGen` statistical sanity test (mean ~0, std within bounds, pink spectral tilt roughly -3 dB/oct via a small FFT or a two-band energy ratio). CI running `cargo test` and `cargo clippy -- -D warnings`.

**Linux ALSA / PipeWire quirks.** (S to M) The `f32`-only stream build (section 2) is the first thing to fix. Then: PipeWire exposes 48 kHz by default (exercises the resampling path), some ALSA `default` devices only accept I16, and `cpal` on JACK needs the `jack` feature. Document `libasound2-dev` as a build dependency in the README.

**Headphone detection.** (M, platform specific) Binaural only works on headphones. On macOS this means CoreAudio `kAudioDevicePropertyDataSource` queries (`coreaudio-rs`), on Linux parsing PipeWire or PulseAudio port names. Low value for the complexity; a one-line "headphones required" hint when `b` is pressed is S and covers most of the need.

### 4.2 Structure and duplication

- `SamplePlayer::new` and `from_preloaded` are byte-identical except for `sample.data` vs `sample.data.clone()`. `new` is dead. After the section 1 refactor both disappear in favour of `bake_loop` + `LoopVoice`.
- `samples_dir`/`load_sample` hardcode the three file names; the slot system replaces them.
- `audio.rs::start_audio` is a 350 line closure with ~40 captured mutable locals. The morse signal, click and tick generators (lines 146-182, 313-371) are three copies of the same "sine burst with linear ramps" with different constants. A `ToneBurst { freq, vol, len, ramp, active, sample, phase }` struct with `trigger()` and `next(rate) -> f32` removes about 70 lines and makes the morse sequencer a thin layer over it.
- The per-generator ramp code (lines 254-259) is three copies of a `slew(cur, target, step)` one-liner.
- `AudioState` mixes three kinds of fields: user parameters (volumes, carrier), commands (`pending_*`, `tone_click`, `timer_signal`) and feedback (`noise_vol`, `binaural_freq`, `paused` being set from both sides). Splitting into `Params` (atomics, TUI writes, audio reads) and `Commands` (atomics or a bounded `rtrb`/`ringbuf` SPSC queue of an enum) would make ownership obvious and remove the pending-flag ordering question.
- `main.rs::parse_noise_type` falls back to brown for any unknown string, so `noiz purple` plays brown without a message. A `clap::ValueEnum` gives validation and `--help` output for free.

### 4.3 `tui.rs` (894 lines)

One function, `run_tui`, is 820 lines: state, timer logic, a 540 line `draw` closure, and a 215 line key handler. Suggested split, no behaviour change:

- `tui/state.rs`: `UiState { selected_row, compact, show_help, confirm_quit, timer: TimerState, last_* }` and `TimerState { end, paused_remaining, mode, fired, signal_at }` with `adjust_by`, `tick(now) -> Option<TimerEvent>`, `display()`. The timer state machine is currently spread over five locals and three places in the loop; it is the most bug-prone part (the last fix commit was exactly a timer/pause desync) and the most testable once isolated.
- `tui/theme.rs`: the colour constants (lines 136-145, 168-170) as `const` items or a `Theme` struct, instead of being rebuilt every frame inside the closure.
- `tui/draw.rs`: `draw_sources`, `draw_controls`, `draw_anim` (noise bars, binaural blob, rain drops as three functions), `draw_help`, `draw_quit`. The animation block alone is 160 lines.
- `tui/input.rs`: `handle_key(key, &mut UiState, &AudioState) -> Action`. The `h`/`l` handlers (lines 760-835) are mirror images; a `step(row, dir: i8, shift: bool)` function halves them. The `n`/`b`/`r` cycle handlers duplicate the `ROW_*` branches of the same arms.
- Rendering allocates a fresh `Vec<Line>` with many `String`s every 100 ms; harmless at this size, but a `Theme` and reusable buffers would make the loop cheaper if you add more animation.
- Quit path: `std::thread::sleep(800ms)` on the UI thread after `y` while the 0.65 s fade runs. Works; a non-blocking "quitting" state with the same deadline would keep the UI responsive to a second key.

### 4.4 Error handling

- `Sample::from_wav` returns `Option`, swallowing the reason (missing file, unsupported format, truncated data). A `Result<Sample, SampleError>` with `thiserror` costs little and lets the TUI show why rain is unavailable.
- `lock().unwrap()` everywhere: poison would panic the audio thread. Moot once the mutexes become atomics.
- `start_audio` returns `Result<Stream, String>`; fine for a binary, but an `enum AudioError` reads better in `--help`-style messages and lets `main` distinguish "no device" from "unsupported format".
- `parse_duration` silently treats a bare number as minutes and returns `None` on garbage; the timer is then just absent without feedback. Report invalid durations at startup.
